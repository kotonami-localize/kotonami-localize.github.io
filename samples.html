Machine-translated Japanese: full before & after edits

Three complete sample edits, laid out exactly the way we deliver them: the English source, the machine-translated Japanese, our rewrite, and a short note on every important fix.

All products, names and texts on this page are fictional and were written for illustration. The "machine translation" lines reproduce typical machine-translation mistakes; they are not the output of any particular tool.

1. Steam store page: "About This Game"

A fictional deckbuilding roguelike. This is the paragraph Japanese players read before deciding to wishlist.

English source

Embark on an unforgettable journey through a world shrouded in darkness! As the last Lantern Keeper, you must explore procedurally generated islands, collect powerful relics, and defeat terrifying monsters. Every run is different. Build your deck, upgrade your lantern, and discover the secrets of the Endless Night. Can you bring the light back?

Machine translation

暗闇に包まれた世界を通して忘れられない旅に乗り出しましょう！最後のランタンキーパーとして、あなたは手続き的に生成された島々を探索し、強力な遺物を収集し、恐ろしいモンスターを倒さなければなりません。すべての実行は異なります。あなたのデッキを構築し、あなたのランタンをアップグレードし、エンドレスナイトの秘密を発見してください。あなたは光を取り戻すことができますか？

Kotonami

闇に閉ざされた世界で、忘れられない旅が始まる。
最後の「灯守(ひもり)」として、訪れるたびに姿を変える島々を探索し、強力な遺物を集め、恐ろしい魔物を打ち倒せ。
プレイするたびに展開が変わるローグライク。デッキを組み、ランタンを強化し、「終わらない夜」の秘密を解き明かせ。
この世界に、もう一度光を灯せるか――。

What it says now: "In a world sealed in darkness, an unforgettable journey begins. As the last Lantern Keeper, explore islands that change every time you visit, gather powerful relics and strike down terrifying monsters. A roguelike where every playthrough unfolds differently. Build your deck, power up your lantern and uncover the secrets of the Endless Night. Can you light this world once more?"

Notes on the key fixes

  1. すべての実行は異なります: "run" was translated as running a program. To a Japanese player this line means "every execution is different," which makes no sense. We rewrote it as "every playthrough unfolds differently."
  2. 手続き的に生成された: a word-for-word rendering of "procedurally generated." Japanese players don't use this phrase. We describe what it means for the player instead: islands that change every visit.
  3. あなた / あなたの appears four times. Japanese normally drops "you," and repeating it makes the text sound like a textbook or a legal notice.
  4. ランタンキーパー / エンドレスナイト: English names written in katakana carry little meaning or mood for many Japanese readers. We suggest Japanese names. You decide, and we record your choices in a glossary so the store page and the game always match.
  5. The closing question あなたは光を取り戻すことができますか？ reads like a quiz. Japanese store copy often closes on a short, dramatic line ending in a dash.
  6. を通して ("through") is used in the sense of "by means of," which is grammatically wrong here.
  7. We added the genre word ローグライク because Japanese players search for it on Steam. We never add or remove content without telling you. Changes like this are always flagged in the notes, and you can say no.

3. App Store listing: subtitle and description

A fictional habit-tracking app. On the App Store, the subtitle is limited to 30 characters and appears under the app name across the store, so every character counts.

English source

Subtitle: Build better habits, one day at a time

Description: Build healthy routines that actually stick. Track your habits, get gentle reminders, and watch your garden grow as you stay consistent. No ads. No pressure.

Machine translation

サブタイトル:一度に一日、より良い習慣を構築する

説明:実際に定着する健康的なルーティンを構築します。習慣を追跡し、穏やかなリマインダーを受け取り、一貫性を保つにつれてあなたの庭が成長するのを見てください。広告はありません。プレッシャーはありません。

Kotonami

サブタイトル:毎日つづく、かんたん習慣づくり

説明:健康的な習慣が、無理なく身につく。
毎日の記録とやさしいリマインダーで、続けるほど画面の中の庭がすくすく育ちます。
広告なし、ノルマなし。

What it says now: Subtitle: "Easy habit-building that lasts, day after day." Description: "Healthy habits that take hold without strain. With daily tracking and gentle reminders, the garden on your screen grows the longer you keep going. No ads, no quotas."

Notes on the key fixes

  1. 一度に一日: "one day at a time" translated word by word. It isn't a Japanese expression, and it uses up the 30-character subtitle without saying anything. The new subtitle is 15 characters and includes 習慣 (habit), the word Japanese users actually search for.
  2. 構築する: "build" as in building a system or a database. Habits aren't "constructed" in Japanese. They are formed (身につく, 習慣づくり).
  3. 追跡する: "track" as in tracking a parcel or following a suspect. For habits, Japanese apps say 記録 (record).
  4. 一貫性を保つにつれて: "as you stay consistent" turned into abstract, academic wording. 続けるほど ("the longer you keep going") is how people really talk about it.
  5. 広告はありません。プレッシャーはありません。 sounds like a list of denials. 広告なし、ノルマなし is the short, confident style Japanese app pages use. ノルマ (a quota) is the everyday word for the kind of pressure you mean.

6 signs your Japanese was machine-translated

You don't need to read Japanese to check for most of these. Open your Japanese page and use your browser's find (Ctrl+F) to look for the patterns.

1. "あなた" everywhere

あなた means "you." Natural Japanese uses it rarely. If it appears in almost every sentence, the text was translated word for word.

2. Katakana copies of English names

Long katakana strings such as エンドレスナイト are English sounds with little meaning for many Japanese readers. Key names usually deserve a Japanese version.

3. The wrong meaning of a word

背中 (a body part) for "Back," 在庫 (shop stock) for "Inventory," 実行 (running a program) for a game "run." These come from missing context.

4. Stiff "〜することができます"

"You can do X," translated literally, makes every sentence long and formal. Natural copy is shorter and says it directly.

5. One concept, several words

保存 on one screen and セーブ on another, or two names for the same item. Japanese players read this as a sign of low care.

6. English punctuation and spacing

English commas and periods (, .) in place of 、 and 。, or spaces between Japanese words, look foreign at a glance.
